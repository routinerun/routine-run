# Routine Run in five minutes

[The full method](method.md)

**Your head is for decisions, not for remembering the order of steps.**

You do the same things again and again: a morning, leaving the house, closing a deal, a
Friday review. Routine Run holds how you do them, walks you through them, and gets a little
better every time you do.

## Five words

- **Routine**: how you do something. Written once, used many times. *"Morning."*
- **Run**: one time you do it. *"This morning's morning."* One routine can have many runs,
  even several at once.
- **Step**: one thing to do next. Not a box to tick, just the next thing.
- **Trigger**: what starts a run for you. A time, a place, a Focus, a message from another
  app.
- **Keep** or **Only here**: after a run, what you changed either goes into the routine for
  next time (**Keep**), or was just for today (**Only here**).

## The ideas, in plain words

**1. Let the phone remember, you decide.** The order, the times, what happened last time: the
app holds all of that. You do the steps and make the calls.

**2. One step at a time.** You see the step you are on, not the whole list. A long list tempts
you to grab the quick, easy thing instead of the next one.

**3. Only what is going.** Keep in sight only what you are actually doing now. Swipe the rest
away; it keeps its place and waits.

**4. Start with what you can do; the routine learns from you.** Begin with the steps you already
do. Changed something today? Say **Keep** and next time starts
with it. Say **Only here** and it stays today's. Three runs in, the routine is what you really do. Something new?
One step with a list in it; split a part off only when it needs a time or a place of its own.

**5. Change the order when you need to.** Bins before keys today? Fine. The routine gives the
usual order; the run follows the day. Where the order really matters, the routine can hold it.

**6. Put it off, don't drop it.** Can't do a step now? Move it one step on, to the end, to a
time or to a place. Or **Decide later**, and sort it out when you have a minute.

**7. Catch now, sort later.** On the go, one tap sends a thought to **Inbox**. Twenty quiet
minutes on Sunday, and the inbox becomes steps in the right routines.

**8. When there is no date, it asks.** "Has the parcel arrived?" every few hours, until you say
yes. You stop checking in your head.

**9. Dates that move with life.** "Ten days after the offer is accepted." When the date moves,
everything hanging on it moves too.

**10. No ticks, no scores, no streaks.** A missed day breaks nothing. A step is done when you move on. Done it somewhere else already? Walk past it when it comes up;
the same step in two routines is fine. What you learned in
it becomes the next steps, or a new routine. Nothing to win, nothing to lose.

**11. It asks before what can't be undone.** Everything that can be undone just happens, with
an undo. The few things that can't, it asks about first, so you actually read the question.

**12. Steps become habit, or the machine's.** What you now do without thinking can shrink to a
single line: cut the step down, say **Keep**, and the run stops spelling out what you already
know. One small habit at a time, then the next. What the phone can do, hand over a step at a
time: first on a press, then as the run arrives. The ideal process is the one you do not see:
it grows, peaks, and then goes into the background.

## Like jazz, not a score

A checklist is a piece of classical music: played the same, every time, as written. That is
right for a pre-flight check. Routine Run is jazz: a tune you know, played the way today
needs. Be water. **Let your flow fly.**

## Who it is for

People who are not looking for ways to fill free time, but for ways to win it back: to get
what fills the day out of their head, and finally make room for what has been out of reach. That can be what you already do, or a new habit that starts as one action and grows run by run.
